\section{Objectives}
\label{sec:objectives}

\todo{State the general objective and the five specific objectives agreed with
the supervisor:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{General objective.} Study similarity-learning techniques for
        music analysis and for building graphs of relationships between songs.
  \item \textbf{O1.} Learn latent representations with siamese networks from
        spectrograms, audio embeddings and acoustic descriptors.
  \item \textbf{O2.} Compare them experimentally with classical classification
        techniques and similarity measures, and with alternative deep models

  \item \textbf{O3.} Build similarity graphs between songs and analyse their
        structure.
  \item \textbf{O4.} Model gradual or ambiguous musical relationships with
        fuzzy logic.
  \item \textbf{O5.} Evaluate everything on FMA and MagnaTagATune with
        Recall@K, MAP and nDCG, plus the human judgements of MTT.
\end{itemize}
Then state the two levels the evaluation is organised into (do not call probing
a level: it is a diagnostic used inside N1, see below):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{N1 --- comparative.} Do different representations induce
        significantly different similarity graphs, in quality and in
        structure? This is the level of the siamese-input comparison, the
        siamese-versus-classical comparison, and of asking whether the size of
        the representation gap predicts the size of the graph gap.
  \item \textbf{N2 --- absolute and of validity.} Does a representation induce
        a good similarity graph on its own, against chance and against
        published references, and do the conclusions drawn with the genre
        proxy hold up against the human triplet judgements of MagnaTagATune?
\end{itemize}
Probing classifiers over frozen embeddings (following \cite{Castellon2021} and
\cite{Yuan2023}) are a diagnostic reused inside N1, not a third level: they
measure what a representation makes linearly decodable, which is a different
question from whether its graph is good.}

\section{Research questions}
\label{sec:research-questions}

\todo{State the main research question (PQ) and the six sub-questions
(SQ1--SQ6) that operationalise it:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{PQ.} Do differences between musical representations (siamese
        networks with different inputs, a triplet network, a CNN, MERT,
        classical techniques) translate into significant differences in the
        structure and the quality of the resulting similarity graphs?
  \item \textbf{SQ1 (N2).} Does each representation, on its own, induce a good
        graph, against chance and against published references?
  \item \textbf{SQ2 (N1).} Do graphs from different representations differ
        significantly in quality and structure?
  \item \textbf{SQ3 (N1).} Does the size of the difference between
        representations predict the size of the difference between graphs?
        Does the difference between models exceed the noise between seeds?
  \item \textbf{SQ4 (N2).} Do the conclusions drawn with the genre proxy hold
        up under the human judgements of MTT?
  \item \textbf{SQ5 (N1) --- siamese focus.} Which siamese input (spectrogram,
        audio embedding, acoustic descriptors) gives the best representations
        and graphs? Do siamese networks outperform classical classification
        techniques, classical similarity measures, the CNN and MERT?
  \item \textbf{SQ6 (N1/N2) --- fuzzy.} Does modelling similarity and genre
        membership gradually capture ambiguous musical relationships better
        than the crisp approach? Does the gain depend on the representation?
\end{itemize}
State that SQ6 crosses both levels, comparing crisp and fuzzy graphs for each
representation, and that the criterion used to answer PQ itself is stated in
advance in the evaluation protocol chapter (Section \ref{sec:pq-criterion}).
Add the question that motivates the whole ground-truth design: does the genre
proxy measure the same thing as perceived similarity?}
