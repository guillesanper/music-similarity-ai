\section{Agreement with human judgements}
\label{sec:triplet-protocol}

The second family of ground truth does not use genre at all. It is the set of
similarity constraints derived from the odd-one-out votes of MagnaTagATune
\cite{Law2009}, exactly as Wolff et al.\ derive them \cite{Wolff2012}: a vote
for the outlier $k$ of a presented triplet $(i, j, k)$ yields the two
constraints $d(i,j) < d(i,k)$ and $d(j,i) < d(j,k)$, and when two constraints
contradict each other the opposing votes are subtracted, so that only the net
majority survives, with the number of votes left as its \emph{margin}. The
derivation, and the counts it reproduces, are in Section \ref{sec:triplets}:
860 constraints of total margin 6898, over 337 triplets of clips and 993
distinct clips. The code asserts every one of those counts, so a different
file cannot pass unnoticed.

\paragraph{Metric.} A constraint $(s, p, n)$ is satisfied when
$\cos(s, p) > \cos(s, n)$, on embeddings normalised to unit length, as
everywhere else. An exact tie counts as one half, the chance value, so that a
representation that cannot tell two clips apart neither gains nor loses
anything. The headline number is the plain mean over the 860 constraints,
because that is the figure the literature reports and the only one a reader
can compare with published work. Two variants are reported next to it as
secondary readings: the \emph{vote-weighted} mean, in which each constraint
counts as many times as its margin, so that constraints people agreed on more
weigh more; and the mean over the 642 constraints (282 triplets) with a margin
of at least 2, which leaves out the 218 constraints that rest on a single net
vote. The metric needs no gallery and no relevance function: it looks at three
clips at a time.

\paragraph{Uncertainty.} The constraints of one triplet share their three clips
and their voters, so they are not independent. Every interval is therefore a
95\,\% percentile bootstrap over the 337 triplets, not over the constraints
(Section \ref{sec:uncertainty}). A difference between two representations is
the paired version: both are scored on the same resampled triplets, and the
interval is on the difference. The vote-weighted variant needs no weighted
bootstrap: each constraint is repeated as many times as its margin before
resampling, so drawing a triplet brings in its constraints with their weights
and the mean of a resample is the weighted mean of what was drawn. This
arrangement is ours; it is exact because margins are whole numbers of votes.
The $p$-value of a difference comes from the same resamples, by inverting the
percentile interval at zero, with one added to each tail so that a finite
number of resamples never certifies an exact zero; the smallest value it can
take with $B$ resamples is $2/(B+1)$. Holm's correction is applied over the
headline variant of every pair the experiment declares; the vote-weighted and
margin variants are reported uncorrected. The correction is
\emph{provisional} until the family holds every representation of the report,
and the final tables use $10\,000$ resamples instead of $1000$: with $1000$,
a family of more than 25 comparisons could not survive Holm whatever the data
said.

\paragraph{Scaling on MagnaTagATune.} Every MagnaTagATune clip is extracted and
embedded, not only the 993 that carry a constraint, and the standardisation
(and the PCA, where an experiment uses one) is fitted on all of them rather
than on a training split. This is legitimate here and nowhere else: no label
takes part in the triplet metric, so unsupervised statistics fitted on the
evaluated clips leak nothing. It is preferred to reusing the statistics fitted
on FMA because MagnaTagATune carries nothing above 8\,kHz, which would shift
every vector in the same direction, and the cosine is not invariant to a
shift. The decision is ours. On FMA, where genre is the relevance signal, the
scaler is always fitted on training alone. Nothing about a representation is
re-selected on these constraints either: \texttt{mert} keeps the layer chosen
on FMA validation (Section \ref{sec:model-selection-protocol}), because
choosing it again here would select on the evaluation itself.

\paragraph{Reference points.} Chance is 0.5. A random embedding, run with
three seeds, is the negative control and shows how far chance alone moves the
figure. Without any audio, Jaccard similarity over the 188 tags satisfies
0.570 [0.546, 0.593] of the constraints, tying on 70\,\% of them out of
sparsity, and ``same genre'' agrees with the vote 0.901 [0.822, 0.961] of the
time, but only on the 101 constraints (12\,\%) where it separates the two
candidates at all (Section \ref{sec:genre-proxy}). In the literature, a bag of
audio words over MFCC with its first and second derivatives, not trained on
the constraints, satisfies 63.6 to 64.6\,\% \cite{Karamanolakis2016}, and
metrics learned on the constraints themselves, under cross-validation, reach
69.5 to 75.6\,\% \cite{Wolff2012}. No representation in this work is trained
on MagnaTagATune, so the first range is the comparable one; the second is an
upper reference, not a target.

\paragraph{Limitations.} Four of them bound what this evaluation can say.
(1)~\emph{Bandwidth}: the audio is 16\,kHz mono MP3, so nothing above 8\,kHz
reaches any representation, while FMA is decoded at 22\,050\,Hz; MagnaTagATune
is resampled to 22\,050\,Hz so that one extractor serves both corpora, and to
24\,kHz from there for \texttt{mert}. (2)~\emph{Duration}: the clips last
about 29.1 seconds and are padded to 30, so the last window of every clip is
partly silent. (3)~\emph{Domain}: the Magnatune catalogue leans towards
classical, new age and electronic music, far from the eight genres of FMA, so
agreement here measures transfer as much as similarity. (4)~\emph{Power}: 860
constraints in 337 triplets give an interval of roughly $\pm 3$ to 5 points on
each representation, and about $\pm 4$ on a paired difference. That separates
a baseline from chance, and a baseline from a model ten points better, but not
two variants a few points apart. The human ceiling is also well below 1: the
most-voted clip takes on average 70.7\,\% of the votes of its row
(Section \ref{sec:triplets}). Three of the 25\,863 clips are empty MP3 files in
the published archive and cannot be extracted by any representation; none of
them appears in a constraint.

\section{Model selection}
\label{sec:model-selection-protocol}

Any decision that could be tuned to the test split is instead made on FMA
\emph{validation}: early stopping, hyper-parameter grids, and -- the first
case the code implements -- MERT's titular layer. Each of the 13 hidden
states \texttt{mert} exposes is L2-normalised and ranked by Recall@10 on
validation only (\texttt{musicsim.extractors.mert.select\_titular\_layer});
the winner is the layer every later stage uses, never re-chosen against test:
layer 6 (of 0--12), R@10 = 0.0548 on the 800-item validation gallery
(\todo{13-layer figure: Recall@10 against layer index}). The same rule governs every later
selection: F1's $\alpha$, F3's cut-offs, and the GNN's binary-versus-intensity
objective are all chosen on validation, stated here once rather than repeated
at each one.

\section{Invariants}
\label{sec:invariants}

The whole evaluation rests on three rules that the code enforces rather than
assumes. Rows and identifiers stay aligned, and every cross-reference goes
through the identifier, never through a position. Anything fitted, the scaler,
the PCA and the probes, is fitted on the training split alone. Every source of
randomness is seeded from the single seed the run records.
